What I learned: Through this assignment, I learned how to effectively apply both the ideal and approximate diode models to analyze real-world circuits. 
I also gained a better understanding of how parallel diodes behave, specifically how a diode with a lower turn-on voltage (like Silicon at 0.7V) will
clamp the voltage and prevent a parallel diode with a higher threshold (like GaAs at 1.2V) from turning on.

Main difficulty: The main difficulty was paying close attention to the circuit diagrams to catch the "trick" configurations.
It was challenging at first to spot when a diode was actually reverse-biased due to the specific orientation of the battery terminals, 
or recognizing that two diodes placed head-to-head in anti-series would act as a complete open circuit.
